How questions are processed
  1. Raised: while reading a page, the extractor may record something the page leaves unanswered as a question, tied to concepts on that page. A question with nothing concrete behind it is dropped; a near-duplicate of an open one is merged into it.
  2. Checked: every later page is compared against up to five open questions (the one the probe was aimed at first, then the nearest).
  3. Advanced or answered: a separate LLM pass says whether the page bears on a question (advanced) or settles it (answered). The page shows up in the question's evidence trail. It can never raise a question.
  4. Aimed: some probes start from a question's own concepts (the “aim at questions” setting), and the walk then chooses where to go next.
  5. Let go: a question nothing touches loses pressure and goes dormant; the pool is capped.
